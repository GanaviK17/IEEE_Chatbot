# IEEE 802 Standards - IEEE 802.11 Wireless LAN (Wi-Fi)
Source URL: https://standards.ieee.org/standard/802_11-2020.html

IEEE 802.11 is the globally recognized standard family for Wireless Local Area Networks (WLAN), developed and maintained by the IEEE 802 LAN/MAN Standards Committee. In consumer electronics and telecommunications, products implementing IEEE 802.11 are universally branded as Wi-Fi.

What is IEEE 802.11?
The IEEE 802.11 specification defines the Media Access Control (MAC) layer and Physical (PHY) layer protocols for implementing wireless computer communication in various unlicensed radio frequency bands, primarily 2.4 GHz, 5 GHz, and 6 GHz.

Key Amendments and Evolutions:
- IEEE 802.11b (1999): First widely adopted Wi-Fi standard operating in 2.4 GHz delivering up to 11 Mbps.
- IEEE 802.11a (1999): 5 GHz band operation providing speeds up to 54 Mbps.
- IEEE 802.11g (2003): 2.4 GHz band providing speeds up to 54 Mbps with backwards compatibility.
- IEEE 802.11n (Wi-Fi 4, 2009): Introduced MIMO (multiple-input multiple-output) antennas, dual-band operation, and speeds up to 600 Mbps.
- IEEE 802.11ac (Wi-Fi 5, 2013): High-throughput 5 GHz wireless networking with wider channels (up to 160 MHz) and multi-gigabit capability.
- IEEE 802.11ax (Wi-Fi 6 / 6E, 2021): High-efficiency wireless supporting OFDMA, MU-MIMO, and the 6 GHz spectrum.
- IEEE 802.11be (Wi-Fi 7, Extremely High Throughput): Ultra-wide 320 MHz channels and multi-link operation delivering throughput up to 46 Gbps.

Other Essential IEEE 802 Standards:
- IEEE 802.3: The global standard for wired Ethernet local area networks.
- IEEE 802.15: Wireless Personal Area Networks (WPAN) including Bluetooth (802.15.1) and Zigbee (802.15.4).
