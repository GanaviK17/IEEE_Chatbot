The Standards Behind the Spectacle: How IEEE SA Helps to Enable the World’s Game

IEEE Standards Association (IEEE SA)

11 June 2026

4 minute read

Every four years, the world’s premier international football tournament captures the attention of billions. Fans tune in for the drama on the pitch—goals, strategy, and national pride—but behind every match is a sophisticated ecosystem of technologies that make the experience seamless, secure, and globally accessible. From real-time data and AI-assisted officiating to connected stadiums and sustainable infrastructure, IEEE Standards Association (IEEE SA) standards play a critical role in helping to ensure these systems work together reliably and responsibly at scale.

A Connected Game Powered by Standards

Modern international football is more than a sporting event—it is a global digital platform. Broadcast feeds, mobile apps, live statistics, and social media all depend on high-performance networking and interoperable systems. IEEE connectivity standards, such as IEEE 802.11™ (Wi-Fi) and IEEE 802.3™ (Ethernet), enable the movement of massive volumes of data across stadiums, broadcast centers, and fan devices worldwide. And

IEEE 802.1BA™

helps ensure synchronized audio and video for live events.

The scale is extraordinary: the 2022 final drew approximately 1.5 billion viewers globally, underscoring the importance of resilient and efficient communication networks. IEEE standards for media services and data formats further support streaming workflows, enabling consistent, high-quality viewing experiences across platforms.

Cybersecurity: Protecting the Digital Stadium

As stadiums and fan experiences become increasingly connected, cybersecurity becomes essential to the matchday experience. Ticketing systems, public Wi-Fi, payment platforms, and broadcast infrastructure all present potential vulnerabilities if not properly secured.

IEEE SA standards and projects—such as IEEE 1686™ for cybersecurity capabilities and IEEE 802E™ for privacy considerations—help create resilient and trustworthy ecosystems. These frameworks are critical in mitigating evolving threats, such as distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attacks and phishing campaigns, which have targeted past tournaments.

By addressing cybersecurity holistically, IEEE standards help protect not only operational systems but also fan data and the integrity of the event itself.

Data, AI, and Real-Time Decision Making

Today’s matches are driven by data. Advanced tracking systems capture player movements, ball trajectory, and match events in real time. During the 2022 tournament, 12 cameras tracked every player’s body position 50 times per second, generating millions of data points per match.

IEEE standards support this data-rich environment by enabling interoperability across sensors, analytics platforms, and broadcast systems. For example:

IEEE 1451™ and IEEE 2700™ help standardize sensor communication and performance parameters

IEEE P3715™ defines common formats for sharing match data

IEEE P3716™ supports quality checks for tracking technologies

These standards ensure that data can be trusted and used consistently—whether for officiating decisions, performance analysis, or fan engagement.

Artificial intelligence is also transforming the game. AI-supported officiating technologies, such as semi-automated offside systems, rely on standardized data and communication frameworks. IEEE’s work in ethically aligned design (including the IEEE 7000™ series) helps guide the responsible development of these intelligent systems.

Enhancing Officiating and Player Insights

From connected referee systems to advanced performance analytics, IEEE standards underpin innovations that improve both fairness and insight. Wearables, body cameras, and communication tools give referees real-time support while enhancing transparency for fans.

On the player side, technologies such as 3D body modeling and movement tracking help teams assess workload, fatigue, and injury risk. Standards like IEEE P3141™ for body data interoperability help ensure that these insights can be shared and applied consistently across teams and organizations.

As more teams participate—expanding to 48 in 2026—these standardized data frameworks help level the playing field by enabling broader access to high-quality analytics and performance insights.

Sustainability and the Future of Global Events

The expansion of international tournaments also brings sustainability challenges. Increased travel, energy consumption, and infrastructure demands place pressure on host cities and global resources.

IEEE standards contribute to more sustainable event ecosystems through:

Smart energy systems (IEEE 2030.5™)

Grid integration and storage guidance (IEEE 1547.9™)

Green community network frameworks (IEEE 1888™ series)

These technologies support efficient venue operations, reduced emissions, and more resilient infrastructure—key considerations as global events scale in size and complexity.

Building Trust at Global Scale

Ultimately, what connects every aspect of the ‘beautiful game’ is trust: trust in the data, the systems, and the decisions made in real time. IEEE SA standards help provide the foundation for that trust, enabling interoperability, security, and ethical innovation across a highly complex ecosystem.

From the stadium to the smartphone, from the referee’s whistle to the final broadcast, IEEE standards help ensure that the technologies behind the spectacle operate seamlessly and responsibly—so fans around the world can focus on what matters most: the game.
