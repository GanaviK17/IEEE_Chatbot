# IEEE Geographic Structure: Regions, Councils, Sections, Subsections, and Chapters
Source URL: https://www.ieee.org/about/organization/regional.html

IEEE organizes its worldwide activities geographically to ensure members, students, and professionals receive localized support, networking, technical events, and leadership opportunities.

1. The 10 IEEE Regions:
IEEE divides the globe into 10 geographic regions:
- Regions 1 to 6 (United States): Covering Northeastern (R1), Eastern (R2), Southeastern (R3), Central (R4), Southwestern (R5), and Western (R6) United States.
- Region 7 (Canada): IEEE Canada.
- Region 8 (Europe, Middle East, and Africa): Spanning European countries, Africa, and the Middle East.
- Region 9 (Latin America and the Caribbean): Spanning Mexico, Central America, South America, and the Caribbean.
- Region 10 (Asia and Pacific): Also known as R10, covering India, China, Japan, Australia, New Zealand, South Korea, Singapore, and Southeast Asia. Region 10 is one of the largest and fastest-growing regions in IEEE.

2. IEEE Councils:
Councils are formed to coordinate activities across multiple sections within a large country or geographical area. For example:
- IEEE India Council: Coordinates across 12 IEEE Sections across India, supporting national student congresses, AISYWC (All India Student, Young Professionals, Women in Engineering Congress), and nationwide technical initiatives.

3. IEEE Sections:
An IEEE Section is the foundational operating unit of IEEE on a local geographical level. There are more than 340 IEEE Sections globally.
- Responsibilities: Sections organize professional conferences, technical talks, distinguished lecturer programs, awards banquets, and provide guidance, governance, and financial grants to local Student Branches.
- Major Sections in India (Region 10):
  * IEEE Bangalore Section: One of the most vibrant and decorated sections in Region 10, established in 1976.
  * IEEE Bombay Section
  * IEEE Delhi Section
  * IEEE Hyderabad Section
  * IEEE Kerala Section
  * IEEE Madras Section
  * IEEE Pune Section
  * IEEE Uttar Pradesh Section
  * IEEE Kolkata Section
  * IEEE Gujarat Section

4. IEEE Subsections:
When a Section spans a large geographic region, Subsections are established to serve members in specific cities or districts under the parent Section.
- Under the IEEE Bangalore Section, active Subsections include:
  * IEEE Mysore Subsection (IEEE Mysuru Subsection): Serving educational institutions, universities, and industries across Mysore, Mandya, Hassan, and Chamarajanagar.
  * IEEE Mangalore Subsection: Serving institutions in Coastal Karnataka.
  * IEEE North Karnataka Subsection: Serving Hubballi, Dharwad, Belagavi, and surrounding northern districts.

5. Student Branches, Chapters, and Affinity Groups:
- Student Branches (SB): Established at accredited colleges and universities to engage undergraduate and graduate students in technical activities, leadership, and networking.
- Student Branch Chapters (SBC): Technical society units within a student branch (such as IEEE Computer Society Student Chapter, PES Chapter, RAS Chapter).
- Affinity Groups: Local student units for Women in Engineering (WIE), Young Professionals (YP), and SIGHT (Humanitarian Technology).
- vTools Platform (vtools.ieee.org): The official IEEE platform used by Sections, Subsections, and Student Branches to record meetings, officer rosters, annual activity reports, and event registrations.
